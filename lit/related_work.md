# Related Work

*Draft for the paper. Citation keys match `references.bib`. Every cited work was checked against its arXiv, ACL Anthology, proceedings, or publisher page on 2026-10-30.*

## 2.1 Indirect Prompt Injection Benchmarks for Agents

Greshake et al. [greshake2023youve] first showed that LLM-integrated applications can be hijacked by instructions planted in the data they retrieve, rather than in the user's prompt. This threat is known as *indirect prompt injection* (IPI). Since then, several benchmarks have formalized IPI. BIPIA [yi2023bipia] tests LLMs on email, table, QA, summarization, and code tasks with injected content. InjecAgent [zhan2024injecagent] targets tool-integrated agents: it has 1,054 test cases over 17 user tools and 62 attacker tools, and finds that a ReAct-prompted GPT-4 agent is compromised 24% of the time. AgentDojo [debenedetti2024agentdojo] replaces static test cases with a stateful environment of 97 user tasks and 629 security test cases across four suites (Workspace, Slack, Travel, Banking). It scores utility and attacker success by inspecting the environment state after the agent finishes. Agent Security Bench (ASB) [zhang2025asb] extends coverage to 10 scenarios, more than 400 tools, and 27 attack and defense methods, reporting average attack success as high as 84.30%. For web agents, WASP [evtimov2025wasp] finds that simple human-written injections partially succeed in up to 86% of cases. AgentDAM [zharmagambetov2025agentdam] studies a related failure, unnecessary leakage of private data by web agents. All of these benchmarks are written and evaluated in English only. We extend AgentDojo because its tasks are executable and its defenses are already implemented as pipeline components.

## 2.2 Prompt Injection Defenses

Defenses work at several layers. *Prompting defenses* mark the boundary between data and instructions. Spotlighting [hines2024spotlighting] proposes delimiting, datamarking, and encoding untrusted text; AgentDojo implements the delimiting variant, along with a "repeat user prompt" (sandwich) baseline and an LLM-based tool filter [debenedetti2024agentdojo]. *Training-time defenses* teach the model to separate instructions from data: StruQ uses structured queries with reserved delimiters [chen2025struq], and SecAlign uses preference optimization [chen2025secalign]. *Detection defenses* classify untrusted content before the agent sees it. Examples are the DeBERTa-based ProtectAI detector that AgentDojo ships [protectai2024deberta], PIGuard [li2025piguard], DataSentinel [liu2025datasentinel], and Meta's PromptGuard 2 [chennabasappa2025llamafirewall]. *Execution-level defenses* include MELON [zhu2025melon], which re-runs the trajectory with the user prompt masked and flags actions that do not depend on the user's task, and CaMeL [debenedetti2025camel], which separates control flow from data flow and solves 77% of AgentDojo tasks with provable security, against 84% for an undefended system. Detection defenses are the most likely to depend on language. The ProtectAI model card says plainly that the model does not handle non-English prompts [protectai2024deberta]. PromptGuard 2 86M was evaluated on machine translations into eight languages: English, French, German, Hindi, Italian, Portuguese, Spanish, and Thai. Hindi is among them, but Bengali, Telugu, and Romanized or code-mixed text are not [chennabasappa2025llamafirewall]. As far as we know, no published work has tested any of these defenses against Indic-language or code-mixed injections inside an agent loop.

## 2.3 Multilingual and Low-Resource Jailbreaks

A large body of work shows that safety alignment learned mostly in English transfers poorly to other languages. Yong et al. [yong2023lowresource] translated AdvBench prompts into low-resource languages and obtained actionable responses from GPT-4 79% of the time. Deng et al. [deng2024multilingual] found that low-resource languages are about three times as likely to produce harmful output in an "unintentional" setting, and that deliberately combining multilingual prompts with harmful instructions makes the problem worse. Shen et al. [shen2024language] found that unsafe and irrelevant responses are more common for lower-resource languages, and that instruction tuning in those languages helps little. A survey by Yong et al. [yong2025state] estimated that the second most-studied language receives about ten times less safety research than English. The pattern does not carry over cleanly to agents, though. STING [talokar2026sting] reports that in multi-turn agentic misuse across six non-English settings, attack success did not reliably increase for lower-resource languages. This is one reason to measure the effect directly in an agent setting instead of assuming it from chat-based jailbreak results.

## 2.4 Code-Mixed, Transliterated, and Romanized Attacks

Code-switching and non-native scripts are a separate attack surface. Code-Switching Red-Teaming [yoo2025csrt] produced 46.7% more successful attacks than English-only red-teaming. Aswal and Jaiswal [aswal2025cmp] combined Romanized Hinglish code-mixing with phonetic misspellings. Without jailbreak templates, this raised attack success from 0.10 to 0.50 on ChatGPT-4o-mini and from 0.06 to 0.63 on Llama-3-8B-Instruct, and the authors attribute the gain to changes in tokenization. Ghanim et al. [ghanim2024arabizi] report a similar effect for transliterated Arabic and Arabizi. The evidence on Romanization is mixed: IndicJR [pattnayak2026indicjr] finds that Romanized or mixed inputs *lower* jailbreak success in its contract-bound JSON track, with jailbreak success correlating with romanization share (ρ≈0.28–0.32). On the defense side, BanglaShield [kabir2026banglashield] shows that LLMs and guard models often label unsafe Bangla prompts as safe, and that romanization makes this worse. Upadhayay [upadhayay2026hinglish] builds a five-layer detector for stealthy Hinglish prompt injection. All of this work targets single-turn chat or standalone classifiers. None of it embeds code-mixed payloads in tool outputs that an agent consumes.

## 2.5 Indic LLM Safety Benchmarks

Indic safety evaluation has grown quickly since 2025. IndicSafe [pattnayak2026indicsafe] tests 10 LLMs on 6,000 culturally grounded prompts in 12 Indic languages, including Bengali and Telugu, and finds only 12.8% cross-language agreement in safety judgments. SurakshaEval [banerjee2026surakshaeval] provides human-written prompts in ten Indian languages plus English, and lists code-mixed and transliterated settings as future work. IndicSafeEval [mondal2026indicsafeeval] combines persuasion-based jailbreaks across Hindi, Bengali, Marathi, and Punjabi. IndicJR [pattnayak2026indicjr] offers a judge-free jailbreak benchmark over 12 South Asian languages. These benchmarks measure what a model *says* to a harmful user request. They do not measure what an agent *does* when untrusted data instructs it to act.

## 2.6 Multilingual Prompt Injection on Agents and Retrieval Systems

The work closest to ours appeared in 2025–2026. MAPS [hofman2026maps] translates GAIA, SWE-bench, MATH, and ASB into 11 languages, Hindi among them. On ASB, attack success averages 47.3% across non-English languages against 32.7% in English. MAPS includes neither Bengali, Telugu, nor code-mixed text, and does not evaluate injection-specific defenses or vary the injection language separately from the task language. MIPIAD [muhtadi2026mipiad] builds an English-Bangla IPI detector from BIPIA templates and cuts end-to-end attack success from 0.260 to 0.202 in Bangla, but the setting is text-only rather than a tool-using agent. Other multilingual injection studies cover direct (not indirect) injection in Turkish, Russian, and Chinese [uysal2026empirical]; hidden injections in Japanese, Chinese, and Arabic against LLM-based peer review [theocharopoulos2025hidden]; and cross-lingual English-Indonesian injection in a RAG pipeline with query, body, and payload language varied separately [prihananto2026crosslingual]. A single-model pilot on Sarvam-105B [madhusudhanan2026visible] studies IPI with English, Tamil, and Tanglish payloads in delimited untrusted content (24 injected trials, no tools, no defenses). Finally, the BabelDojo project [babeldojo2026] has announced a multilingual extension of AgentDojo. Its candidates include Hindi, Bengali, Tamil, Hinglish, and Romanized Hindi, and its plans include a defense evaluation and an attack-language versus user-language analysis. As of October 2026 it has published no experiments or results. LoWAgent [monjur2026lowagent] shows that web-agent *utility* drops in Bengali and Hindi, but does not study security. Our work differs from all of these in four ways together. It runs an executable tool-using agent benchmark in Hindi, Bengali, Telugu, and Hinglish. It treats script (native versus Romanized) and code-mixing as controlled variables. It crosses task language with payload language in a full matrix. It measures how AgentDojo's built-in defenses and a multilingual detector degrade outside English, using only free-tier models.
